# Frequently Asked Questions

## Profile

### What does Martin do?

I work across data engineering, analytics engineering, analytics and data
architecture.

A consistent theme throughout my career has been taking fragmented or immature
data environments and making them more useful: improving architecture,
modelling data around business concepts, introducing engineering practices,
building trusted analytical datasets and helping organisations use data to make
better decisions.

I have worked both as a hands-on technical practitioner and as a leader building
and managing data teams.

### How would you describe Martin professionally?

I am a data and analytics leader with a strong technical background.

I am comfortable moving between business problems and implementation: working
with stakeholders to understand what they are trying to achieve, designing the
data architecture and models required to support it, and working directly with
SQL, Python, dbt and cloud data platforms where necessary.

I tend to be most useful where an organisation needs both technical depth and
the ability to bring structure to ambiguous data problems.

### What roles is Martin suited to?

My experience fits roles including:

- Analytics Engineering leadership.
- Data and Analytics leadership.
- Senior or Principal Analytics Engineering.
- Senior or Principal Analytics roles.
- Data Architecture.
- Data Engineering leadership.

The exact title matters less to me than the problem, the level of ownership and
the opportunity to do useful work.

### Is Martin a manager or an individual contributor?

I have substantial experience doing both.

I have built and managed multidisciplinary data teams, hired across different
levels, developed people and introduced operating models and engineering
practices.

I have also remained technically hands-on and am comfortable operating as a

I don't see leadership and technical contribution as mutually exclusive.

### Is Martin still hands-on technically?

Yes.

My strongest technical skills are SQL, data modelling and analytics

In recent consulting work I have personally designed and implemented dimensional
models, written SQL transformations, created testing and linting standards,

conversational AI.

### Is Martin more of a data engineer or an analyst?

Both disciplines are part of my background.

I started closer to web and product analytics and progressively moved deeper
into data engineering, modelling and architecture as the systems required to
support good analysis became more important to my work.

That combination is useful because I understand why data is being built as well
as how to build it.

I don't view a technically elegant data platform as successful if it doesn't
help someone answer useful questions or make better decisions.

## Analytics Engineering and Architecture

### What is Martin's approach to analytics engineering?

I see Analytics Engineering as the discipline that turns raw technical data
into reliable representations of business concepts.

That requires more than writing transformations.

It includes modelling, testing, documentation, version control, code review,
CI/CD, observability and clear ownership of business logic.

The goal is to give analysts and other consumers trusted foundations so they
can spend more time answering questions and less time repeatedly reconstructing
the same logic.

### What is Martin's approach to data modelling?

I start with the business rather than the source tables.

I want to understand the entities the organisation works with, the processes
those entities participate in and the questions people need to answer.

From there I can create conceptual and logical models and translate them into
physical models appropriate to the technology and use case.

I make grain explicit and generally favour dimensional models for analytical
work because they provide a simple and understandable representation of
business entities and processes.

### What does Martin mean by source-conformed and business-conformed data?

Source-conformed data reflects the systems that produced it.

Business-conformed data reflects how the organisation understands itself.

A good analytical architecture progressively moves from one to the other.

At EFG this distinction was particularly important because many independent
microservices contained overlapping representations of users, games and other
business concepts.

The analytical model needed to resolve those technical representations into
something the business could use consistently.

### What is Martin's approach to data architecture?

I prefer architecture that matches the organisation's actual requirements,
capabilities and maturity.

I don't believe the most sophisticated technology is automatically the best
choice.

I normally favour clear separation of concerns, low-cost storage, incremental
processing, well-defined analytical models, controlled development processes,
observability and the ability to evolve individual components without rebuilding
the entire platform.

### How does Martin approach technical debt?

I distinguish between technical debt that is merely untidy and technical debt
that creates genuine business or operational risk.

I generally stabilise the highest-risk problems first, introduce enough
measurement to understand the system, and then improve the architecture
incrementally.

At Stonecrop, for example, we could not stop operations while we rebuilt the
entire data platform. I stabilised the existing system, introduced monitoring,
improved database and pipeline performance and then used what we learned to
shape the longer-term architecture.

## Semantic Layers and Governance

### What is Martin's approach to semantic layers?

I see a semantic layer as the contract through which an organisation turns data
into agreed business meaning.

It should describe business entities, processes, metrics, dimensions,
relationships and other semantic information in a form that consumers can use
without needing to understand all the complexity of the underlying systems.

The technology used to implement that contract is secondary to agreeing and
governing the meaning itself.

### Has Martin built a semantic layer?

At EFG I built many of the foundations of a semantic architecture rather than
what I would today describe as a complete standalone metrics layer.

During the Snowflake migration I introduced business-conformed dimensional
models, explicit grain, reusable business logic, governed facts and dimensions,
domain-oriented consumption models, testing, metadata and lineage.

We subsequently worked with a conversational AI vendor to create a semantic
representation on top of that model and had begun golden-query testing when the
wider Data team was made redundant.

In later Apollo work I explicitly designed semantic information and approved
query surfaces for conversational AI.

### How does Martin approach metric definitions?

I separate the **business definition** of a metric from its **technical
implementation**.

The organisation first needs to agree what something means. Only then should we
decide how to calculate it.

I learned the importance of that distinction at EFG when senior stakeholders
spent more than 90 minutes trying to agree on the definition of something as
apparently simple as subscriber count.

Writing the SQL is often the easy part.

### What is Martin's approach to data governance?

I prefer governance embedded in architecture and engineering workflows rather
than governance based primarily on meetings and approval gates.

That includes clear ownership, version-controlled logic, peer review, testing,
CI/CD, appropriate access controls, documented models and observable production
systems.

The goal is to make the safe and consistent way of working the easiest way of
working.

### How does Martin balance governance with self-service?

I want people to have considerable freedom to explore trusted data, create
analyses and ask new questions.

I don't want every analyst independently redefining core business concepts.

I distinguish between **freedom to analyse** and **freedom to redefine shared
business meaning**.

Good governance protects the latter so that the former becomes safer and
easier.

## AI and Data

### Does Martin have AI experience?

My AI experience is primarily at the intersection of AI and data architecture
rather than training machine-learning models.

I have worked on designing analytical data and semantic interfaces that allow
LLMs and conversational agents to interact reliably with business data.

At EFG I worked with a conversational analytics vendor on semantic modelling
and golden-query evaluation.

At Apollo I designed semantic grounding and query guardrails for a
conversational analytics POC over sports-performance and medical data.

### How does Martin think data should be prepared for AI?

I don't think an LLM should simply be pointed at a complicated warehouse and
expected to infer how an organisation works.

The model should be given a controlled representation of the relevant business
semantics.

Depending on the problem, that can include:

- Table grain.
- Metrics and dimensions.
- Business definitions.
- Approved relationships and join paths.
- Temporal rules.
- Units.
- Valid values.
- Aggregation behaviour.
- Business filters.
- Query constraints.

The objective is to reduce the amount of business meaning the model has to
guess.

### What are golden queries?

Golden queries are known business questions with known correct analytical
answers or query patterns.

They provide a way of testing whether a conversational analytics system
continues to interpret the semantic model correctly as prompts, models,
metadata or underlying data structures change.

I began working with this approach during the conversational analytics project
at EFG.
