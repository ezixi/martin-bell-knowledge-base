# Google Analytics Measurement and Instrumentation

## Overview

As Safari began developing and rolling out its next-generation product, the
existing Omniture implementation was becoming increasingly unsuitable.

There were two related problems.

The first was trust. Although the original Omniture implementation had been
technically sound when it was introduced, it had not kept pace with changes to
the existing product. Data quality was widely perceived as poor, and confidence
in the platform had deteriorated.

The second was that we were building a fundamentally new product. The existing
measurement implementation reflected the structure and behaviour of the old
one.

We needed an analytics implementation designed around what the new product was
trying to achieve.

I drove the implementation of Google Analytics for the new platform.

## Starting With a Measurement Plan

I didn't want the implementation to begin by simply adding tracking tags to
pages.

I started by creating a measurement plan that connected the product's
objectives to the behaviours we needed to understand.

From that I identified and codified an event structure representing meaningful
interactions within the product.

The intention was to make the analytics implementation reflect how Product and
the business thought about the new experience rather than simply collect every
interaction because it was technically possible to do so.

The sequence was:
**product objectives → questions → user behaviours → events → analysis**

That gave us a structured measurement framework for the new product.

## Working Directly With Engineering

I wanted to be able to contribute to the implementation rather than handing a
tracking specification to Engineering and waiting for it to come back.

That required me to learn more of the development environment used by the
product team.

I learned **Git, Vagrant and Chef** so I could work with the product codebase
and development environment.

I also developed my **JavaScript** skills so I could add and modify analytics
events directly, and began learning and using **Google Tag Manager** as part of
the instrumentation work.

This allowed me to move between the analytical and implementation sides of the
problem.

I could define what we needed to measure, understand why we needed it, implement
instrumentation and then use the resulting data to understand behaviour.

## Rebuilding Trust in Product Analytics

Changing analytics platforms wasn't simply a technology refresh.

The previous Omniture implementation had accumulated a perception that the data
couldn't be trusted.

A new product gave us an opportunity to start again with measurement designed
into the product rather than continually adapting an implementation originally
built for something else.

The measurement plan and event taxonomy provided an explicit definition of what
we were collecting and why.

This also made conversations with Product more useful because events were tied
back to product objectives and customer behaviour.

## Supporting Growth

Around the same period, Safari's new leadership established a dedicated
**Growth team**, building on the type of acquisition and conversion
optimisation work I had previously been doing.

I supported the team with data and insight.

The new instrumentation allowed us to understand how customers moved through
the product and gave the Growth team evidence they could use when identifying
opportunities and evaluating changes.

This continued the evolution of analytics at Safari from retrospective
reporting towards being directly involved in product and growth decisions.

## From Omniture to a Broader Analytics Capability

The Google Analytics implementation was one stage in a wider evolution of
Safari's analytical capability.

We moved progressively from a legacy Omniture implementation, through Google
Analytics, and eventually towards our own BI and clickstream infrastructure.

The important progression wasn't simply:
**Omniture → Google Analytics → BI/clickstream**

It was the increasing ability of people across Safari to access, understand and
use behavioural data.

That progression is independently reflected in the recommendation I later
received from Safari's VP of Product, who described me as pivotal in helping
Safari become more data-driven and specifically highlighted the move from
Omniture through Google Analytics and into the internal BI/clickstream
capability.

## What I Learned

This project reinforced my view that measurement should be designed around the
decisions a product needs to make.

It is easy to instrument a product by collecting large numbers of events.

That doesn't necessarily create useful analytics.

Starting with objectives and questions forces each measurement to have a
purpose and makes the resulting data easier for Product, Growth and Analytics
to understand.

It also reinforced the value of being technically close to implementation.

Learning the development tooling and contributing instrumentation myself meant
I wasn't dependent on a hand-off between Analytics and Engineering every time
measurement needed to change.

I could understand the business question, translate it into a measurement
design, understand its technical implementation and analyse the resulting
behaviour.

That ability to work across the boundary between **business questions, data
and implementation** has remained a consistent part of how I work.
